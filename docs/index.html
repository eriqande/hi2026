Welcome

Welcome to the Hawaiʻi CKMR Workshop 2026, taught by Mark Bravington and Eric Anderson.

This is our course web page: a clearinghouse for links to the slides, exercises, code, data, and other materials you will need. We will update it as the workshop goes along. Many of the materials will be hosted on the instructors’ GitHub pages or in their GitHub repositories; we will introduce them and add links here as we use them.

Prepare your laptop

Please prepare R and the packages for both instructors’ hands-on exercises before the workshop. R 4.6.x is the recommended starting point, with pre-built packages for a broad range of supported Mac and Windows systems. Mark’s packages also have builds for other R versions, including R 4.5.x on Mac and Windows; see his setup page for the platform/version options.

Installation commands for required packages can be run in the R console. See the following links for explicit commands.

Do some pre-workshop reading

This course is intended to provide a “cold start,” meaning we build up CKMR concepts from their foundations without requiring prior knowledge. Nonetheless, if you wish to read up on CKMR before the workshop, or during your flight to Hawaiʻi, we recommend two readings, each with a different flavor.

  • If you enjoy mathematical statistics and notation, the fundamentals of CKMR are laid out in those terms in Bravington, Skaug, and Anderson (2016). Close-kin mark-recapture. Statistical Science 31:259-274.
  • For a more accessible introduction to CKMR, we recommend Bravington and Carroll (2023). The practical magic of close-kin mark-recapture. CSIRO Publishing (download PDF). To download it, accept Mark’s emailed invitation to his private GitHub repository and sign in with the GitHub account associated with that invitation.

Essential reading: Please read Mark’s 2026 R cribsheet (PDF). Reading this ahead of time can help immensely. Mark has developed an R ecosystem of packages and functions, and he relies on R environments for computational efficiency and syntactic brevity. This may be unfamiliar to some R users. His cribsheet provides an essential introduction.

BYOS — Bring your own species

The workshop agenda includes worked examples for a series of different species that illustrate real-life scenarios. In addition, the extended nature of the course is intended to provide participants with a chance to apply principles of CKMR design to their own species of interest. For such design, it is not necessary to know everything, but we provide brief BYOS guidelines for key pieces of information you’ll want to be armed with.
